# Awareness Levels (Schwartz)

From [course 3](../sources/course-3-ads-transcript.md) (~00:14:00). Five levels of consumer awareness that determine the **language** of your ads.

> "There are five levels of awareness: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, and most-aware."

| Level | Customer state | Ad language |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem | Educate: name the problem. Example: "If you're over 45 and your back's been hurting for weeks — you may have a misplaced disk." |
| **Problem-aware** | Knows the problem, not the solution | Recommend a specific solution and explain why: "You have tweaked-disk syndrome; we recommend this, not ice packs or couch rest." |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows the problem *and* the range of solutions | Differentiate: why your product is different/better (e.g. "show up once a week instead of three," "personal coach you can text," "they pick you up"). |
| **Product-aware** | Knows your specific product | Present the compelling offer. |
| **Most-aware** | Knows everything, ready to buy | Just show up with the offer; "skim off the top of the goodwill created by organic content." |

> "This is not something we actively use when we write ads each time" — but markets shift over time, so check where your market sits awareness-wise before writing.

Pair with [market sophistication](sophistication-stages.md), which is about competitors, not the customer's knowledge.

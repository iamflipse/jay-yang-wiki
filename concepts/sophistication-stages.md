# Market Sophistication Stages

From [course 3](../sources/course-3-ads-transcript.md) (~00:16:54). Awareness levels describe the *customer*; **sophistication** describes the *market* — how many competitors there are and how worn-out the claims have become.

> "There are five main stages of sophistication."

| Stage | State | Ad strategy | Pushup example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No one has made the claim yet | Just make the claim and win | "Do pushups, you'll lose weight." |
| 2 | Competitors show up | Make a **bigger** claim | "Do pushups, lose 50 lbs by tomorrow." |
| 3 | Claims are worn out | Introduce a **mechanism** | "Use the corkscrew pushup to lose 50 lbs." |
| 4 | Competitors copy your mechanism | **Improve** the mechanism | "Corkscrew method + proprietary doctor-trusted pill, 50 lbs in 2 weeks." |
| 5 | Market saturated; claims don't land | **Identify with the person your customer wants to become** — lean into personal brand/story | "I grew up a really fat kid... tried everything... it wasn't until the corkscrew pushup + pill that I lost weight." |

## Identifying your stage

> "Go to the Meta Ads Library, find 20 of your competitors' ads, and write down the claim in each. If they're all making the same claim, you're in stage 3+ and need a mechanism."

## Strategy note: first-mover advantage & the #2 play

- Be a first mover if you can (no competitors → stage 1).
- If you're #2, use it: the 1962 **Avis** campaign — "Avis is number two. Why should you go with us? We try harder."
- "When you have a big behemoth, you can outcompete them by being faster, delivering better results, or caring more."

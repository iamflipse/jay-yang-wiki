# I've Written 60,000 Tweets: How to Write a Great Sentence

A great sentence is the backbone of society as we know it.
Politicians use sentences to get elected into office. Leaders use sentences to inspire their team. Parents use sentences to convince their kids to listen to them. People use sentences to show their love for each other.
I believe everything you want out of life is on the other side of arranging the right words in the right sequence.
The problem is that, especially with AI nowadays, everyone is outsourcing their thinking and writing to it, which is reducing their ability to craft great sentences.
Automate only the skills you're willing to lose.
But that's good news for you and me. If you commit to mastering the art of a great sentence, you'll be miles ahead of your peers.
"Writing great sentences has changed my life:"
- A cold email got me my first internship at beehiiv when I was 16.
- "You Can Just Do Things" became a best-selling book.
- Ads I helped write sold over $100 million in 3 days.
Great sentences have landed me dream jobs. They've landed me dream clients. They've helped me build an audience. They've helped me write a book.
The only thing they haven't done yet is find me a wife.
I've written over 60,000 tweets. That's 60,000 reps at the same skill: squeezing one idea into 280 characters. And you can't compress an idea you don't have.
By the end of this article, you'll know how to take an idea nobody cares about and turn it into a sentence nobody can ignore.

### What is a great sentence?
Step one is to define what a great sentence is.
I think of sentences in a 2x2 matrix:
- True statements that are obvious
- True statements that are non-obvious
- False statements that are obvious
- False statements that are non-obvious
![2x2 matrix](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118704999224242176)
The same idea in each box:
- True and obvious: "You'll never create a masterpiece by accident."
- False and obvious: "Everyone can create masterpieces as long as they believe it."
- False and non-obvious: "Take advice from average people to get non-average results."
- True and non-obvious: "If you make accidental decisions based on arbitrary advice from average people, you will end up with average outcomes at best."
We want to say true things in non-obvious ways.
When I'm writing tweets, I often check them with 2 questions:
1. Is this true?
2. Is this non-obvious?
A lot of tweets are true but obvious. And when something feels like "yeah, I've heard that before," it doesn't trigger much emotion or engagement.
So go one layer deeper into the non-obvious truth. A few examples:
- "You can beat most people by simply deciding what you want and then doing things on purpose to get it. That's it. Most people never even get that far."
- "Few things feel emptier than finally getting something you never stopped to question if you even wanted in the first place."
- "The most dangerous thing isn't a bad life, it's a good life you never chose. You'll fight to escape a bad life. A good one? You'll always find an excuse for never reaching for great."
Pro tip: if someone in your niche would read your sentence and think "that's not true," it fails, no matter how punchy it is.

### What makes a sentence bad?
One of the fastest ways to learn what a great sentence looks like is to study bad ones.
Here are 6 tweets, what's wrong with each one, and how I'd rewrite them.
1. The motivational poster
![tweet screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705002130923520)
- Trite. It sounds like a motivational poster in an elementary school classroom.
- It's not even that motivational, just preachy.
- Formatting note: you never want to split a sentence across lines like that, especially if you end on it. Hit enter twice instead.
Rewrite: "It takes everyone a different amount of time to realize that harder problems are a good thing. It means you have more opportunity to grow. Just like more weight in the gym leads to muscle growth, more responsibility leads to mental growth."
2. The buzzword pile
![tweet screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705004467134464)
- Too many buzzwords: "99%," "mentor," "successes and failures."
- People tune out what sounds familiar.
Rewrite: "The fastest way to accelerate your career is to get in rooms where your life goals are their minimum standards."
3. The fortune cookie
![tweet screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705006522384384)
- I don't even know what this means.
- Too vague and amorphous. It needs context.
- You're not Naval.
Rewrite: "I will never bet against the person who continues to show up every day even when the rewards are not guaranteed. Your potential is determined by the amount of uncertainty you're able to tolerate and how long you can tolerate it for."
4. The textbook
![tweet screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705008879534080)
- Hard to read. Too many commas.
- Too many words that need unpacking. What does "careful, logical reasoning" mean? What does "unbound exploration" mean? What is play?
- Keep everything under a 3rd grade reading level.
Rewrite: "People underestimate the power of creating space for stillness and wandering. Nearly all of my best ideas have come when I was away from my laptop doing something completely unrelated to what I was working on."
5. The undefined abstraction
![tweet screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705011169587200)
- More buzzwords that need to be defined. What is urgency? How does it compound?
- It lacks specificity.
Rewrite: "The fastest way to spot a successful person: watch how quickly they make decisions, respond to messages, and turn ideas into action. Speed is king."
That rewrite went live on a 9-figure CEO's account and did 326K views the first time we posted it.
6. Contrast for the sake of it
![tweet screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705013602332672)
- The em dash reads as AI.
- Buzzwords make people tune out again: grow, comfort, dirt.
- It's the "you don't blah, you blah" template with nothing underneath.
- I have no idea what I'm supposed to do with this information.
- How do you get dragged through the dirt and stand up clean? That doesn't make sense.
Don't use juxtaposition and contrast for the sake of it. The sentence still has to make sense.
Rewrite: "Your inability to start without certainty will hurt your potential far more than your lack of talent. The reason entrepreneurs get paid the most is because they take on the most risk, uncertainty, and responsibility."
![rewrite screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705016026570752)

### How do you fix a weak sentence?
When a sentence is generic, you have 2 options:
- Chunk up: go broader, to the bigger principle.
- Chunk down: add details the reader can picture.
I chunk down more often. "Move fast" is vague. "Watch how quickly they make decisions, respond to messages, and turn ideas into action" gives the reader 3 actions they can picture someone doing.
![chunk up vs chunk down](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705018547343360)
3 fixes I've made, labeled by the problem:
Too concise to land.
Before: "You don't need accountability. You need integrity."
After: "Most people don't need more accountability, they need more integrity. Accountability = doing the right thing because someone else is watching. Integrity = doing the right thing because you are watching."
Motivational poster.
Before: "Do one more when it hurts. That's the rep that counts."
After: "In the gym, the last rep hurts the most and matters the most. In business, the hardest conversation hurts the most and pays the most."
Too many undefined words.
Before: "Small margins build massive gaps."
After: "The older I get, the more I realize everything is details. Calling leads within 60 seconds vs. 60 minutes. One extra follow-up vs. stopping at three. Testing 10 headlines vs. settling for the first one."
For every vague word, ask yourself, "What the hell do I mean?"
Then run the sentence through my 8 writing rules:
1. Below a 3rd grade reading level.
❌ We utilize leverage to gain higher returns on our equity checks.
✅ We borrow money so we can buy bigger stuff (and make more) than we could with our cash alone.
2. Present voice.
❌ When you're creating your sales page
✅ When you create your sales page
3. Active, not passive.
❌ The body was carried out of the room.
✅ We carried the body out of the room.
4. Strong verbs over adverbs. Adverbs usually mean lame verbs.
❌ We shut the door really hard.
✅ We slammed the door.
5. Usually one comma. If there are 2 commas, cut the middle or make 2 sentences.
❌ We tried to escape but the man, with the red mask, chased us until we lost our breath.
✅ The red-masked man chased us until we lost our breath. We were trapped.
6. Positive language.
❌ Don't stop.
✅ Keep going.
7. Cut redundant words.
❌ We simultaneously left the building at the same time.
✅ We left the building at the same time.
8. Cut unnecessary words. Also cut very, super, way, and actually.
❌ He was able to get out of his car.
✅ He got out of his car.
![8 rules screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705021328216064)
Screenshot those rules and use them as a checklist before you post.

### What are rhetorical devices?
While I wish there was a silver bullet to craft a great sentence every single time, that's not how the world works.
What I can give you is a toolbox.
Rhetoric is the art of transferring information from one brain to another in the most effective way possible. Rhetorical devices are the techniques writers use to persuade, get an emotional reaction, or make a message more memorable.
![rhetoric definition](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705024180314112)
Here are the 9 I use the most:
1. Juxtaposition: put 2 things side by side that don't usually go together.
❌ Reading old books is important for your creativity.
✅ If you want new ideas, read old books.
2. Contrast: set what the reader thinks they need against what they need.
❌ When you have trouble sleeping, it's often due to your lack of purpose.
✅ You don't need more sleep, you need a better reason to get out of bed.
3. Before/After: show where you were, then where you are now.
❌ Fear is a sign to lean in.
✅ I used to be terrified of putting myself out there. But every great thing in my life has been a result of embracing discomfort and doing it anyway. Fear is a sign to lean in.
4. Repetition: repeat a word or phrase so the point hits harder each time.
❌ We must act quickly, with determination and unity.
✅ We must act now. We must act decisively. We must act together.
5. Alliteration: start nearby words with the same sound.
❌ Fake friends lie to you.
✅ Fake friends make false promises.
6. Parallelism: give every item in a list the same shape.
❌ She likes reading, to write, and skiing.
✅ She likes reading, writing, and skiing.
7. Metaphor/Simile: explain an idea by comparing it to something the reader already knows.
❌ Overthinking is bad. You need to take real action.
✅ Trying to think your way out of overthinking is like trying to snort your way out of a crack addiction.
8. Antithesis: put opposite ideas in one balanced sentence.
❌ Want to stand out? Get started.
✅ The quickest way to get ahead is to get started.
9. Anadiplosis: end one phrase with a word, then start the next phrase with that same word.
❌ Fear is dangerous. Anger is destructive. Hate corrupts you.
✅ Fear leads to anger. Anger leads to hate. Hate leads to suffering.
![9 devices screenshot](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705026915028992)
The rule: every device has to mean something. "You don't blah, you blah" with nothing underneath is one of the most common AI tells. If a device costs you clarity, go back to the plain sentence.

### The 4 advanced moves
These are the 4 I lean on the most when I write for founders.
1. Antithetical parallelism.
Parallelism: you repeat a sentence structure ("Lack of X has killed more dreams than lack of Y"), which creates rhythm and clarity.
Antithesis: you contrast 2 opposing ideas ("focus" vs. "talent") inside that structure to show which one matters.
So instead of saying "Focus is more important than talent," say: "Lack of focus has killed more dreams than lack of talent."
That one did 252K views on the CEO's account.
![antithetical parallelism](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705029570068480)
2. The rule of three.
Ideas in groups of 3 are more memorable and satisfying. Three creates rhythm and a sense of completeness: beginning, middle, end.
Use 3 lines to build tension and expectation. Then use a fourth line to break the pattern and deliver the payoff.
So instead of saying "You have to risk what you have to get what you want," you can say:
"You can't get rich if you never risk losing money. You can't get loved if you never risk getting rejected. You can't get strong if you never risk getting injured. The price of the life you want is the risks you're avoiding."
![rule of three](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705032074080257)
3. Aspirational identity framing.
This is when you describe a behavior through the lens of a group people want to belong to:
1. Name a desirable identity (successful person, high performer, disciplined, rich).
2. List the behaviors that signal that identity (decide fast, respond fast, take action).
3. Readers compare themselves to that standard.
4. If they aspire to that identity, they lean in and engage.
So instead of saying "Move fast and get things done quickly," you write: "The fastest way to spot a successful person: watch how quickly they make decisions, respond to messages, and turn ideas into action."
![aspirational identity](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705034791903233)
4. Specificity x utility.
Clear instructions feel useful, and useful content gets saved and shared.
1. Start with a common situation people struggle with (fear, doubt, bad days).
2. Give one specific action that improves that situation.
3. The reader leaves feeling they can apply it today.
For example: "Pro tip: If you feel restless before bed, write down the tasks you plan to do tomorrow. Listing them gives your brain a sense of closure and makes it easier to relax. I call it 'closing the loop.'"
![specificity x utility](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705037467955200)

### How do you write more great sentences?
Once you find a true idea that resonates, say it again in different ways.
Twitter is just saying 1 thing 1,000 different ways.
There are 2 ways to remix a sentence:
1. Same structure, different topic. "Rich people buy time. Poor people buy stuff" becomes "Winners get better. Losers get bitter."
2. Same topic, different structure. "Rich people buy time. Poor people buy stuff" becomes "If you want to build wealth, buy time. If you want to stay poor, buy stuff."
![remix 2 ways](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705039913164801)
I think of it as More → Better → New. In business, the highest risk-adjusted return comes from doing more of what works, then making it better, and only then trying something new. The same applies to sentences.
For the CEO I wrote for, it looked like this:
- "Sooner or later you realize everything comes down to your ability to stick with the plan despite whatever emotions you may feel" did 361K views.
- A later batch said it again: "You become an adult when you realize everything eventually comes down to your ability to stick with what you said you'd do despite whatever emotions you may feel." That one did 288K.
- The speed tweet did 326K views the first time. When we reposted it, it did another 180K.
![More Better New](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705042559807488)
Most of your audience never saw the first version anyway. And the ones who did usually need reminders more than new lessons.

### Exercises to do today
Exercise 1: Collect great sentences.
Start a swipe file. Every time a sentence stops your scroll, makes you laugh, or makes you buy, save it. Then write down why it worked: which box of the 2x2 it lives in and which device it uses.
Exercise 2: Run the 2x2 on your last 10 posts.
Put each one in a box. Every "true but obvious" sentence gets pushed one layer deeper. Every "false" sentence gets cut.
Exercise 3: Name the failure.
Take 5 weak sentences (yours or anyone's) and name what's wrong with each: motivational poster, buzzwords, too vague, too many commas, undefined words, or contrast for the sake of it. Then rewrite each one by chunking down.
Exercise 4: One idea, 5 ways.
Pick 5 core ideas you believe. Write each one 5 different ways, each with a different device from the toolbox. That's 25 sentences.
Exercise 5: Practice on X.
X gives you quick feedback loops. Post a sentence, see what happens, and keep the winners in a Hall of Fame so you can say them again in 90 days.
![exercises](https://x.com/Jayyanginspires/article/2118705056652644600/media/2118705045055438848)

### So what does the system look like?
1. Say true things in non-obvious ways. Check every sentence: is it true, and is it non-obvious?
2. Learn the failures by name. Motivational posters, buzzwords, fortune cookies, textbooks, undefined abstractions, and contrast for its own sake.
3. Chunk down. Ask "what the hell do I mean?" and add details the reader can picture.
4. Run the 8 writing rules. 3rd grade reading level, active voice, strong verbs, one comma.
5. Make every rhetorical device mean something. If it costs you clarity, cut it.
6. Say one true idea 1,000 ways. More, then better, then new.

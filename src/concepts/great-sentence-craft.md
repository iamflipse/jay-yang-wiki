# Great Sentence Craft

From [X article "I've Written 60,000 Tweets: How to Write a Great Sentence"](../sources/x-article-2118705056652644600.md). Jay's 60,000-tweet practice, distilled.

## What a great sentence is

A great sentence is the backbone of society: politicians, leaders, parents, and people all use sentences. "Everything you want out of life is on the other side of arranging the right words in the right sequence."

The 2x2 matrix of statements:

- **True + obvious** — "You'll never create a masterpiece by accident."
- **True + non-obvious** — the target. "If you make accidental decisions based on arbitrary advice from average people, you will end up with average outcomes at best."
- **False + obvious** — "Everyone can create masterpieces as long as they believe it."
- **False + non-obvious** — "Take advice from average people to get non-average results."

> We want to say true things in non-obvious ways.

Jay checks every tweet with 2 questions: **Is this true? Is this non-obvious?** If it reads "yeah, I've heard that before," go one layer deeper. And if the reader would think "that's not true," it fails no matter how punchy.

## The 6 bad-sentence failure modes (with rewrites)

1. **The motivational poster** — trite, preachy. Rewrite: "It takes everyone a different amount of time to realize that harder problems are a good thing... more weight in the gym leads to muscle growth, more responsibility leads to mental growth."
2. **The buzzword pile** — "99%," "mentor," "successes and failures." Rewrite: "The fastest way to accelerate your career is to get in rooms where your life goals are their minimum standards."
3. **The fortune cookie** — vague, amorphous. Rewrite: "I will never bet against the person who continues to show up every day even when the rewards are not guaranteed."
4. **The textbook** — too many commas, too many undefined terms. Rewrite: "People underestimate the power of creating space for stillness and wandering."
5. **The undefined abstraction** — buzzwords without definitions. Rewrite: "The fastest way to spot a successful person: watch how quickly they make decisions, respond to messages, and turn ideas into action. Speed is king." (326K views on a 9-figure CEO's account)
6. **Contrast for the sake of it** — em dashes read as AI, "you don't blah, you blah" with nothing underneath. Rewrite: "Your inability to start without certainty will hurt your potential far more than your lack of talent."

Fix: when a sentence is generic, **chunk up** (the bigger principle) or **chunk down** (details the reader can picture). Jay chunks down more often. For every vague word: "What the hell do I mean?"

## The 8 writing rules

1. Below a 3rd grade reading level.
2. Present voice.
3. Active, not passive.
4. Strong verbs over adverbs.
5. Usually one comma.
6. Positive language.
7. Cut redundant words.
8. Cut unnecessary words (very, super, way, actually).

> Screenshot those rules and use them as a checklist before you post.

## The 9 rhetorical devices

Juxtaposition, contrast, before/after, repetition, alliteration, parallelism, metaphor/simile, antithesis, anadiplosis.

> The rule: every device has to mean something. "You don't blah, you blah" with nothing underneath is one of the most common AI tells. If a device costs you clarity, go back to the plain sentence.

## The 4 advanced moves

1. **Antithetical parallelism** — repeat a structure, contrast two opposing ideas. "Lack of focus has killed more dreams than lack of talent." (252K views)
2. **The rule of three** — 3 lines build tension, a 4th line breaks the pattern and delivers the payoff: "You can't get rich if you never risk losing money... The price of the life you want is the risks you're avoiding."
3. **Aspirational identity framing** — name the identity people want, list the signaling behaviors.
4. **Specificity x utility** — clear instructions get saved and shared.

## More → Better → New

> Twitter is just saying 1 thing 1,000 different ways.

The highest risk-adjusted return: do more of what works, make it better, only then try something new. Real numbers: "stick with the plan" idea did 361K views, the reworded version 288K; the speed tweet did 326K, the repost another 180K.

> Most of your audience never saw the first version anyway. And the ones who did usually need reminders more than new lessons.

## Exercises to do today

1. **Swipe file** — collect great sentences and write down why they worked (2x2 box + device).
2. **Run the 2x2** on your last 10 posts: push "true but obvious" one layer deeper, cut the false.
3. **Name the failure** on 5 weak sentences, then chunk down and rewrite.
4. **One idea, 5 ways** — 5 core ideas × 5 devices = 25 sentences.
5. **Practice on X** — post, measure, keep winners in a Hall of Fame and repost in 90 days.

Trust Recession Content

From X article "The Trust Recession: How to Make Content That Builds Authority and Converts". For educational creators and business owners who use content to grow, not to entertain.

The asset changed

  • AI made content cheap: a LinkedIn post, thread, or YouTube script in under a minute. Lower barrier to entry → more supply, and most of it sounds the same.
  • "The asset used to be attention. But now attention is cheap." Anyone can buy attention with a controversial post, trend, or skit.
  • The true asset now is trust.

We are in a trust recession. There's more content than ever and less reason to believe any of it.

When trust is scarce, the people who have it win by a lot.

What trust is

Trust is belief with evidence. If you have belief without evidence, that's called faith.

Trust means people believe that listening to you will net them positive results. If earned before the sell, leads convert higher (pre-sold), customers pay more and stay longer, and they give you the benefit of the doubt.

The trust loop

Tell people to do stuff → they listen → they get good results → they tie the result to you. Next time you speak, they're more likely to comply.

Examples that run the loop:

  • Martha Stewart — decades of recipes: women baked, got compliments, and associated the good feeling with Martha, so they bought her cookbooks.
  • Parents — "drink milk and you'll grow tall" for years; the positive results built deep trust.
  • Content — someone reads an article, runs the PTL framework, gets clarity on brand positioning; the result is tied to Jay, so the next framework is more likely to be tried.

The question to ask: What step-by-step directions can I give my audience for them to follow and get positive results?

Procedural beats declarative

  • Declarative knowledge: what something is, why it matters. Procedural knowledge: how to do it, the steps.
  • Most internet content is declarative, so it skips the trust loop — there's nothing to do, so no result, no association.
  • Declarative: "Consistency is the key to growing on LinkedIn." You nod and keep scrolling.
  • Procedural: "Every Sunday, I block off 2 hours and write 2 weeks of content..." You can do that this Sunday — and if it works, who do you think of?
  • Pro tip: for educational content, use What / Why / How. What and why set it up; the how is what the reader can go do — that's where trust gets built.
  • Lower the perceived effort: the smaller the first step, the more people take it, the more people get results they tie back to you.

Why AI content makes you blend in

  • AI learned from the internet, and most of the internet is declarative, so it gives you the average: "Be authentic. Provide value. Stay consistent." Anyone could have written it.
  • "The messenger changes the message" — you bring your stories, mistakes, and results to an idea.
  • The Name Test: cover your name and photo; if someone couldn't tell it was you, it's too generic to post.

Earned secrets

An earned secret is something you only know because you paid for it — with time, reps, mistakes, money, or clients — so you can't Google it.

  • Writing about others builds an audience; writing about yourself (stories and learnings) builds authority.
  • Example: the 10,000-word "2027 Marketing Playbook" essay drove business because it took 6 years to learn those lessons — the 6 Ps, the content multiplication system, and the Why They Buy sheet all came from real work.

Collecting directions: pain points

Ask your audience — every pain point is a set of directions waiting to be written. 4 ways to collect:

  1. DM new followers.
  2. Run Q&A posts (be specific about the topic).
  3. Read your comments (a comment like "This won't work for me, I'm coming back from an injury" becomes the next post).
  4. Run free workshops; list every question from the transcript.

One pain point ("trying to land my first client") becomes 5 posts: 3 mistakes before you pitch; the exact way Jay landed his first client; the mistake that cost it; the hot take on portfolios; 5 signs of underpricing. 50 pain points = a few hundred pieces of procedural content.

Only talk about what you know

Don't make content on things you're not an expert in — the trust loop flips: bad result tied to you, less compliance next time. Two gates before posting:

  1. Do I know this super well, have I done it myself more than once?
  2. Do I have the proof (a result, mine or a client's)? If either is no: go get the reps and proof, or leave the topic to someone who has them. (Example: Publish30 — Jay had only ~7 months of LinkedIn writing, so he partnered with Kevin Shim for algorithm questions.)

The 2 banks → content

Every trust-building content piece needs 2 ingredients: a reason to listen (credibility) and something to go do (framework).

Exercise 1: The credibility bank

Write down every accomplishment, result, milestone, and number in one doc; pull from it when writing hooks. Jay's sample: weekly newsletter for 5 years, 60,000+ tweets, 500K+ audience, two businesses to six figures in 8 and 9 days, 2 best-selling books. Prompts when stuck: Results, Reps, People, Builds, Firsts and fasts. Rules: use the credibility that backs up the topic (newsletter stat for newsletter posts, not tweet count); lead with your own proof over name-dropping; keep adding to it.

Exercise 2: The frameworks bank

Frameworks are written-down mental models / SOPs / checklists AI can't copy, because the shortcut only comes after doing the work. Jay's: PTL, the 6 Ps, the 5 Cs, the 3 Rs + Hall of Fame, the story bank, the North Star filter. To create a framework in 5 steps: pick a repeated task → write down every step → cut to the 3–7 steps that move the result → give it a name (a name is a handle) → hand it to someone and verify the result.

Pairing and multiplication

Credibility + framework = valuable content.

In PTL terms: credibility shows power, the framework builds trust (they listen, they get a result), and the story of how you learned it builds likeness (they see themselves in where you started). 10 credibility points × 10 frameworks = 100 pairings; run each through the 3 R's (repost, remix, repurpose) and you have content for years. Formats: the how-to, the big brother/sister post, the public breakdown, the client story, the mistake post.

Turning trust into customers

  • Give your best stuff away; give, give, give, then ask — on X, promote about 1 in 15 posts.
  • One CTA: one CTA, one avatar, one offer. Say who it's for, what to do, when, and how.
  • It shows up as prospects booking calls that reference your posts, and founders asking how you're getting inbound.

Recap: the trust-building system

  1. Attention is cheap; the asset now is trust.
  2. Trust = belief + evidence.
  3. Trust builds in a loop: direction → listen → good result → association.
  4. Make procedural content; give people something to DO.
  5. Don't let AI write your insights — it gives the average, and the average blends in.
  6. Only talk about what you know super well and can prove.
  7. Build the credibility bank and the frameworks bank.
  8. Pair them: credibility + framework = valuable content.

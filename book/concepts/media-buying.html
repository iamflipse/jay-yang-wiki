Media Buying Principles

From course 3 (~00:31:51). "I am not a media buyer" — but these are the "golden BBs" (rules of thumb) learned over the years.

Principle 1 — Optimize for AOV / LTGP, not CPL

"We don't optimize for cost per lead; we optimize for average order value / lifetime gross profit per customer."

Money Models launch test (course 3, ~00:34:02):

CampaignCPLAOV / LTGP
Optimized for CPL~$5~$20 LTGP (bought the book, no upsells)
Optimized for ROAS / LTGP / AOV$17$189 AOV (bought book + workbooks/upsells)

Cheap leads ≠ good leads.

Principle 2 — 15–30 creative variations per $10K of spend

"For every $10K we spend in ads, we want about 15–30 different creative variations so we don't fatigue our ads as we scale." This is why kaleidoscoping (5 hooks / 1 body / 3 CTAs per ad) matters.

Principle 3 — Volume without quality is a waste

Don't iterate by swapping shirts/backgrounds/flipping the camera. "In 2026 the copy is the targeting." Find a winning angle (e.g. stay-at-home moms: "making money so your kids will be proud of you" vs. "making money so you can stay with your kids" — presence > pride) and make many variations of that angle.

Principle 4 — Stop rebuilding campaigns

"Meta typically wants 50 conversions in a 14-day window. Give your ads time to breathe and the algorithm time to figure out who's best for your ads."

Where to find hooks (5 sources, course 3 ~00:24:52)

  1. Your previous winning ads (Meta Ads Library).
  2. Competitors' ads (Meta Ads Library).
  3. Your own organic content — test angles for free, then boost the winners.
  4. Other people's free content.
  5. Outlier tweets from "money Twitter":

    "When Elon Musk takes a bathroom break and opens his phone, he's not scrolling Instagram, he's on Twitter." Example: a 400K-view tweet ("Have you ever had a job where you thought none of this matters?") became the hook: "If you've ever sat at your desk and thought none of this matters, this video is for you."

Hook quality rules (course 3 ~00:26:46)

  • Specificity sells (Claude Hopkins): instead of "Are you broke?" → "Do you turn your phone brightness down when you open your bank app so others can't see your balance?"
  • The hook must be a promise that opens a curiosity gap — not a bare statement ("Your salary went up 3% and rent went up more than that" is not a hook).
  • Describe the moment so the reader thinks "that's exactly me."
  • Be specific in hooks, broader in bodies.
  • "We state the facts and tell the truth. We don't write lies; we manipulate reality so the copy is better."
  • Outcomes must be visualizable: not "you'll sleep better" but "our customers fall asleep in under 10 minutes on average."

2026 targeting note (course 3 ~00:03:02)

Meta's "Andromeda" AI system does most of the targeting: the copy is the targeting. 70–80% of spend runs to broad targeting and lets Meta route by the text/visuals you provide. Meta rewards ads that keep people on the platform (podcast-style, organic-feeling creative).
